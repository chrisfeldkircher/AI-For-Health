\begin{abstract}
We revisit the ComParE 2017 Cold sub-challenge on the URTIC corpus to ask a narrower, more useful question than benchmark chasing: starting from challenge-grounded baselines, what actually improves with modern frozen speech models, and which apparent gains survive stronger speaker-aware and robustness controls? Our historical positive system pairs a frozen WavLM-Large representation with an audit-derived per-layer prior and constrained late fusion. Across 10 alternative pseudo-grouped devel partitions it attains UAR $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$, compared with $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$ for a matched ComParE-2016 $+$ LR control. A later independent audit found that the Train-centroid IDs used to construct those devel partitions fragment Development-local ECAPA structure; these partitions therefore quantify partition sensitivity but are not established speaker-disjoint estimates. We consequently do not extrapolate their canonical $0.7111$ point to the hidden test. The methodological result is the correction itself: speaker-proxy representations and held-side group assignments require separate validation, and whole-official-side transfer is the defensible performance check when true speaker IDs are unavailable.
\end{abstract}
