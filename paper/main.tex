\begin{abstract}
We revisit the ComParE 2017 Cold sub-challenge on the URTIC corpus to ask a narrower, more useful question than benchmark chasing: starting from challenge-grounded baselines, what actually improves with modern frozen speech models, and which apparent gains survive stronger speaker-aware and robustness controls? Our positive system pairs a frozen WavLM-Large representation with an audit-derived per-layer prior --- layer-weight initialisation by per-layer cold gain minus speaker gain, the most transferable modelling component here --- and a constrained, audit-gated late fusion of low-dimensional handcrafted feature groups. We report it shadow-first: across 10 alternative speaker-disjoint devel partitions the multi-K ensemble attains UAR $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$, and under the \emph{same} strict speaker-grouped protocol a classical ComParE-2016 $+$ LR control attains $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$, so the frozen-model pipeline is substantially stronger by $\Delta\,{+}0.109$ shadow-mean UAR on a matched protocol (a same-protocol LR control, not a tuned SVM; \S\ref{sec:disc}). On the single fixed canonical partition the system reaches $0.7111$, adjacent to the 2017 hidden-test baseline of $0.710$; we treat that as one favourable partition rather than an expected-test result, and note that contextualising the hidden-test number through our measured devel partition variance ($\sigma_{\text{shadow}} \approx 0.016$) assumes comparable hidden-test variance. The multi-K ensemble is promoted only because it cleared the same pre-registered shadow-admission gate that a classical K=3 fusion candidate failed. The strongest contribution is methodological: a shadow-split trustworthy-evaluation protocol plus a negative-control discipline set (M8--M19) under which cross-speaker mixup, speaker-masked contrastive learning, and gradient-reversal adversaries all fail to beat a simpler cold-CE bottleneck within the tested frozen-backbone scope. The paper is best read as a structured, audited account of what works, what does not, and how small-data paralinguistic cold detection on URTIC should be evaluated.
\end{abstract}
