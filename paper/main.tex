\begin{abstract}
We revisit the ComParE 2017 Cold sub-challenge on the URTIC corpus to ask a narrower and more useful question than benchmark chasing alone: starting from challenge-grounded baselines, what actually improves with modern frozen speech models, and which apparent gains remain credible under stronger controls? Our main positive system combines a frozen WavLM-Large representation with an audit-derived layer prior and constrained late fusion with selected handcrafted acoustic groups. The best K=2 system reaches devel\_test UAR $0.7037 \pm 0.0060$, and a multi-K ensemble reaches $0.7111$ on the canonical split. A 10-way shadow-split analysis yields $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$, showing that the canonical-vs-2017 gap remains within partition variance; under the same strict speaker-grouped protocol, a classical ComParE-style baseline reaches $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$ shadow-mean UAR. The strongest contribution is methodological rather than leaderboard-driven: speaker-grouped evaluation, negative controls, and robustness checks show that several plausible de-confounding interventions, including cross-speaker mixup, contrastive de-confounding, and adversarial speaker removal, do not improve over a simpler cold-CE bottleneck within the tested frozen-backbone scope. The paper is therefore best read as a structured account of what works, what does not, and how small-data paralinguistic cold-detection systems on URTIC should be audited.
\end{abstract}
