\section{Dataset and Evaluation Design}
\label{sec:data_eval}

\subsection{URTIC}

URTIC contains German read and spontaneous speech recorded in quiet rooms and
released as 16-kHz chunks. Official Train, Development and Test contain 9,505,
9,596 and 9,551 chunks, respectively. Train has 970 cold chunks and
Development 1,011, giving an approximately 10\% minority class. Each official
partition contains 210 participants; Train and Development each contain 37
cold and 173 non-cold participants~\cite{schuller2017interspeech}. Cold status
is derived by thresholding a self-reported illness-severity item. Test labels
are withheld, and the course evaluation returned scores for at most five
submitted prediction files.

Following the original challenge, and to keep results comparable across
course teams, we report unweighted average recall,
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{UAR}=\frac{1}{2}\left(
 \mathrm{Recall}_{\C}+\mathrm{Recall}_{\NC}\right).
 \label{eq:uar}
\end{equation}
It gives cold and non-cold recall equal weight despite class imbalance. UAR
does not, however, determine a clinically appropriate threshold or reveal the
operating point. We therefore also report accuracy, precision, F1 and the two
class recalls where available.

\subsection{How the protocol changed}

The clearest symptom appeared before any audit. Early models validated on
chunk-level splits that did not separate speakers reached implausible internal
scores: a Random Forest over 361 handcrafted acoustic summary statistics
reached 0.9581 internal UAR, and an RBF SVC over pooled self-supervised
features reached 0.9907. Neither transferred. On the official Development
partition the same systems returned 0.5744 and 0.5942, against a 0.5 floor.
No cold classifier separates this task at 0.99; the official challenge
baseline is 0.710~\cite{schuller2017interspeech}. What those splits measured
was speaker identity, which fixes the health label exactly in a
cross-sectional corpus. Two unrelated model families collapsing to near chance
under a speaker-respecting boundary is the effect this paper is about.

Our initial experiments used different internal splits and repeatedly compared
models on a nominal \texttt{devel\_test} subset. A static audit found 45 of 79
experiment cells referencing that subset and 42 containing comparison or
decision markers. It had ceased to be a one-shot holdout. Fusion weight and
threshold selection were also based on a \texttt{train\_threshold} subset with
only three cold pseudo-speakers, while reported uncertainty was mainly across
model seeds rather than held-out participants.

We attempted to repair this by clustering ECAPA embeddings into $k=210$
pseudo-speakers and using grouped folds. Side-local KMeans and HDBSCAN agree
well in raw embedding space (all-recording adjusted Rand index
$0.856/0.829/0.879$ on Train/Development/Test), so ECAPA does contain stable
speaker-correlated structure. The crucial later audit asked a different
question: do labels produced by assigning Development recordings to
\emph{Train} centroids remain valid inside Development? On the canonical
within-Development boundary, 201 of 210 independently recovered
Development-local groups crossed the boundary and 9,399 of 9,596 recordings
belonged to a crossing group. Zero overlap under the transferred IDs was true
only by construction. We therefore retract the old ``speaker-disjoint''
description but do not invent a numerical leakage correction: the audit
invalidates the interpretation, not a known number of UAR points.

Our final evaluation logic is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}. All architecture
and threshold choices are made within the fitting side. The locked model is
then evaluated on an untouched whole official side, so pseudo-groups are not
used to split the evaluation population. Reversing Train and Development gives
a second domain-transfer direction. Side-local groups are used only for
fitting-side out-of-fold (OOF) threshold estimation and uncertainty
resampling.
