\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Five submissions, one narrow UAR band}

Table~\ref{tab:test} reports the hidden-Test results. The best submission was
G4 plus path signatures at 0.6582 UAR. The two global/local deep systems were
within 0.002 UAR of it, while the full spread across all five submissions was
only 0.0362. None reached the 2017 organizer reference of 0.710.

UAR alone hides clinically relevant behavior. Submissions 3 and 5 differ by
only 0.0015 UAR, but their accuracies are 0.558 and 0.771 and their F1 scores
0.468 and 0.580. Thus two systems can balance cold/non-cold recall similarly
while making very different numbers of positive predictions. CORAL changed
UAR only marginally relative to Submission 4, but the observed accuracy and F1
were higher. Because this comparison also includes a finite Test sample and a
fixed calibration path, it is evidence of an operating-point change, not a
causal proof that CORAL universally helps.

\subsection{Evaluation-independent G4+CQT evidence}

The later diagnostics support complementarity between the compact G4 and CQT
branches (Table~\ref{tab:corrected}). In repeated Train-only grouped CV, equal
fusion beats G4 in all six split repetitions and improves the mean by 0.0216
UAR. The paired inferred-cluster interval $[-0.0157,0.0549]$ crosses zero, so
we call fusion the better expected-score candidate rather than a proven
subject-level winner.

Whole-side transfer exposes directional asymmetry. Fusion is clearly best for
Train$\rightarrow$Development, whereas CQT alone is best for
Development$\rightarrow$Train. Nevertheless, fusion exceeds G4 in both
directions and by 0.0405 in the bidirectional mean. Paired proxy-cluster
intervals for this bidirectional fusion gain are $[0.0090,0.0698]$ with the
Train-fitted $k=210$ uncertainty map and $[0.0055,0.0752]$ under a pooled
$k=420$ sensitivity map. Since true identities are unavailable, these are
proxy-cluster intervals rather than subject-level confidence intervals.

Thresholds estimated only from fitting-side OOF predictions raise the
bidirectional fusion mean from 0.6683 to 0.6787. The corresponding paired
proxy-cluster interval, $[-0.0053,0.0270]$, crosses zero. We therefore regard
calibration as a modest expected-score choice, not a statistically established
improvement.

\subsection{What did not survive stronger controls}

The historical WavLM system reached 0.7111 on one pseudo-grouped Development
boundary, but the group-overlap audit invalidates its speaker-disjoint
interpretation. In nested Train-only outer CV, the tested WavLM-Large and
HuBERT-Large heads reached 0.5519 and 0.5640 UAR, respectively, compared with
0.5919 for G4 on the same protocol. These results do not show that foundation
models fail generally; they show that our tested frozen heads did not justify
their complexity under the corrected selection boundary.

Several apparently reasonable refinements also failed. A learned logistic
stack destroyed G4+CQT complementarity: on the same pessimistic split, learned
stacking scored 0.5295 whereas fixed equal fusion at threshold zero scored
0.6076. Under the historical protocol, test-time augmentation reduced the
WavLM ensemble from 0.7090 to 0.6683. Cross-speaker waveform splicing was more
than 0.99 detectable even for a within-proxy-speaker self-splice control,
revealing an operation artifact. Conservative embedding mixup gave a small
UAR change without reducing the historical speaker probe; stronger mixup hurt
UAR. Speaker-masked contrastive heads and gradient-reversal adversaries did
not jointly improve cold UAR and the proxy-speaker diagnostic. These are
scoped negative results: the proxy-label audit prevents a universal claim
about the intervention families.

Table~\ref{tab:negative} separates the observation from the inference. This
distinction matters because a failed implementation does not falsify an entire
method family, while a matched control can still identify why that
implementation was unhelpful.
