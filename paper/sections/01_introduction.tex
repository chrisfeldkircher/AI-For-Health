\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Detecting whether a speaker has a cold from short audio clips is the kind of paralinguistic task where modern foundation-model representations should help and where the standard practice for fine-tuning them creates two persistent problems. The first is the speaker shortcut: in small-data corpora with a few hundred speakers and a binary label that correlates partially with voice identity, a classifier trained end-to-end on a foundation-model representation can score well on a per-utterance speaker recognition probe and only modestly on the actual paralinguistic task~\cite{huckvale2017cold,schuller2017interspeech}. The second is the lack of a clean methodology for measuring whether a proposed de-confounding intervention has done anything: speaker probes vary across substrates, dimensionality, and probe architecture, and an apparent ``probe top-1 dropped from $X$ to $Y$'' result can be an artefact of the bottleneck the intervention introduced rather than de-confounding signal.

Rather than treating URTIC as a fresh novelty benchmark, this paper uses the ComParE 2017 Cold sub-challenge~\cite{schuller2017interspeech} as a structured revisit. We begin from ideas that were already reasonable in the 2017 participant ecosystem, use them to build challenge-grounded baselines, and then ask three questions: do modern frozen speech representations add useful signal, does constrained late fusion add complementary information, and which apparent gains survive stronger speaker-aware and robustness-oriented controls?

Within a frozen-backbone setting and a modest compute budget ($\sim$8.5\,k chunks, $k{=}210$ pseudo-speakers), the positive system combines a frozen WavLM-Large~\cite{chen2022wavlm} representation with a layer-weighted classification head on cached pooled statistics, a per-layer cold-vs-speaker audit, and a late-fusion stage that admits low-dimensional handcrafted feature groups only when they improve the cold task without obvious speaker-leakage regressions. Around that system, we test three de-confounding families that are intuitively plausible on this task: cross-speaker augmentation, supervised contrastive learning with speaker-masked positives, and gradient-reversal speaker adversaries. None improves over the simpler cold-CE bottleneck within the tested scope, which makes the negative results part of the main story rather than a side note.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We argue three contributions, in increasing order of transferability.

\textbf{C1: An audited historical system from frozen speech representations.} A frozen WavLM-Large head with an \emph{audit-derived} layer-weight prior plus constrained K=2 late fusion reaches canonical devel\_test UAR $0.7037 \pm 0.0060$ (per-seed, $N{=}5$), while the multi-K ensemble attains $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$ across 10 alternative pseudo-grouped devel partitions. A matched ComParE-2016 $+$ LR control attains $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$. These measurements establish a same-partition model difference, but a later independent audit found extensive Development-local proxy-group overlap across the historical boundaries; they are not presented as speaker-disjoint estimates or as expected hidden-test performance.

\textbf{C2: A corrected evaluation protocol for small, speaker-confounded paralinguistic corpora.} Because the 4students release omits speaker IDs, we test the ECAPA-TDNN representation independently of the resulting group assignments. Side-local raw-space clustering is reproducible across methods, but Train-centroid assignments fragment held official sides. We therefore use side-local groups for within-side calibration and whole-official-side transfer for the load-bearing performance check; historical shadow splits are retained only as partition-sensitivity diagnostics.

\textbf{C3: An audit-driven account of what survives the tested controls.} Cross-proxy mixup, speaker-masked contrastive learning, and gradient-reversal adversaries do not beat the simpler cold-CE pipeline within the historical matched partitions. Because the held-side group assignments were later shown to fragment Development-local structure, these are scoped negative results rather than categorical speaker-deconfounding closures. Their payload is the failure-mode instrumentation---self-splice controls, bottleneck-confound checks, discriminator diagnostics, and the requirement to audit transferred proxy labels independently---not a universal claim that the intervention classes cannot work.
