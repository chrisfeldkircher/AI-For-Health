\section{K=3 extensions --- G\_egemaps\_full and HuBERT-base}
\label{app:k3}

\subsection{K=3 with full eGeMAPSv02 (G\_egemaps\_full)}
\label{app:egemaps}

Standalone cold-LR UAR for full eGeMAPSv02 is $0.5384$. Historical K=3 UAR is $0.6801 \pm 0.0097$ ($\Delta-0.0236$ versus K=2), with four of five seeds at an extreme $\beta^*$. This boundary-heavy pattern is associated with non-admission in the tested sweep; it does not identify how the fusion architecture represents the candidate.

Full eGeMAPSv02 did not improve this historical fusion, while the selected G3/G6 subsets were already present. This supports retaining the selected subsets within the project but does not isolate dimensional dilution or prove that the omitted dimensions are speaker-specific.

\subsection{K=3 with HuBERT-base mean-pooled (M14 pre-flight skip)}
\label{app:hubert}

\textbf{Extraction protocol.} \texttt{facebook/hubert-base-ls960} (12 transformer layers + 1 input layer, 768-d hidden) without any fine-tuning. For each of the 19{,}101 train + devel chunks, frame-level activations from layers $L \in \{0, \dots, 12\}$ are mean+std+skew+kurt-pooled along the time axis after stripping the padding mask, giving a per-chunk fp16 tensor of shape $[13, 3072]$ ($13 \cdot 4 \cdot 768$). Cache lives at \path|cache/facebook_hubert-base-ls960/pooled/{stem}.pt|. End-to-end extraction took 2.5 min on GPU at 133 chunks/sec --- 12$\times$ faster than initially budgeted because HuBERT-base is small and URTIC chunks are short. The cold-LR substrate is the layer-mean across 13 layers of the 4 stats, giving 3072-d per chunk; this avoids the overfit risk of a full-stack 39936-d LR at $C{=}1.0$ on the $\sim$8.5k train\_fit samples.

\textbf{Standalone cold-LR result.} HuBERT mean pooling scores $0.5396$ standalone UAR. Because this fell below the within-project pre-specified $0.55$ screen, the K=3 sweep was skipped, saving about 5 minutes. The skipped fusion outcome is unobserved; this row demonstrates application of the compute policy, not predictive validity.

\textbf{Follow-up hypotheses.} Mean pooling, layer weighting and backbone differences are all possible contributors to the WavLM--HuBERT gap. A learned layer-weighted HuBERT head subsequently reaches $0.6217 \pm 0.029$ standalone but still fails historical K=3 admission (Appendix~\ref{app:k3_hubert_lw}). This sequence does not yield a causal percentage decomposition, and candidate--anchor correlation was not measured. HuBERT-large mean pooling remains future work.

\textbf{Remaining variants.} HuBERT-large mean pooling, discrete-token histograms and nonlinear heads remain untested. They should be prioritised only after the corrected evaluation protocol is in place.

\section{$\alpha$-axis sweep details for A5.5 (data-level mixup)}
\label{app:a55}

\subsection{Audio splicing v1 (M8 self-splice control)}

Original A5.5 Plan A used 200 ms crossfades and three same-cold, different-proxy-group partners per anchor. A splice detector reached UAR $0.998$; the within-proxy-group self-splice control reached $0.990$. Their near equality indicates that the operation itself dominates detectability in this design, so partner selection alone was unlikely to rescue it. This is a proxy-group control, not a true-speaker mechanism test.

\subsection{Embedding mixup --- both $\alpha$-axis endpoints fail differently}
\label{app:a55_alpha}

\section{A7c disc-ceiling pre-flight and v1$\to$v2 progression}
\label{app:a7c}

\subsection{A7c v1 --- over-conservative fail-fast}

The first A7c version stopped on a train--held discriminator gap above $0.7$ before examining held-boundary accuracy. Because that rule prevented the planned $\lambda$ sweep, its verdict is not retained. Version 2 proceeded after reporting both train and held values.

\subsection{A7c v2 --- corrected M13 ordering}

Version 2 reports train top-1 $1.0000$, held-boundary top-1 $0.0871$ and their $+0.91$ gap, then runs the pre-specified $\lambda$ sweep. The $0.080$ continuation threshold is project-specific.

\textbf{Regularised discriminator for v2.} To reduce the v1-style overfitting noise during adversarial training, the v2 discriminator adds dropout $0.3$ on the hidden layer and weight\_decay $10^{-3}$ on the AdamW optimiser. This brings the regularised disc's devel top-1 down to $\sim 0.071$ (from v1's $0.0871$) on the same substrate.

\textbf{$\lambda$-sweep result (A7c v2).} Across $\lambda_{\max}\in\{0,0.01,0.03,0.1\}$, class UAR and the historical proxy-probe metrics stay within seed variation of $\lambda=0$. This is a negative result for the tested DANN configuration, not evidence of substrate-wide adversarial resistance.

The v1-to-v2 sequence motivates reporting held-boundary ceiling and train--held gap before interpreting the sweep. Other datasets require independently chosen continuation criteria.

\section{K=2 ensemble calibration / stacked weighting (M15 + M16)}
\label{app:k2_calibration}

Four reweighting strategies tested on the per-seed K=2 fused-logit matrix $[N_{\text{chunks}}, 5]$ (Section~\ref{ssec:ensemble_defence}). Per-variant devel\_test UAR and the per-seed locked-$\beta^*$ at $\{6, 8, 8, 8, 12\}$ are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:k2_calibration_app}. The LR-stacking variant produced the worst K=2 result tested in the entire project ($0.6137$), with learned weights $\{+1.57, -1.85, +1.18, -2.89, +2.68\}$ that include sign-flips on 2 of 5 seeds --- consistent with overfit on $n{=}973$ \texttt{train\_threshold} ($\sim 92$ cold examples) for an unconstrained 5-feature LR. The UAR-grid-search variant overfit the train\_threshold metric: best weights $(0, 0, 0.8, 0.2, 0)$ use only seeds $\{7, 999\}$, train\_threshold UAR went from $0.6529$ (mean-logit) to $0.6648$ (grid) ($+0.012$ train gain), but devel\_test went from $0.7090$ to $0.7049$ ($-0.004$ devel loss). The isotonic ablation produced $0.7064$ ($\Delta$ $-0.0026$ vs mean-logit) --- the residual delta is $\tau$-grid resolution mismatch (raw fused-logit grid is 321 points in $[-4, +4]$ at step $0.025$; calibrated probability grid is 197 points in $[0.01, 0.99]$ at step $0.005$; the optimal raw-logit $\tau^* {=} -1.375$ does not map exactly to a calibrated grid point).

\textbf{M15 verification (with caveat).} Argmax-UAR is invariant under monotonic transforms of the score by construction --- if $f$ is monotonic, $\argmax_\tau \text{UAR}(\mathbf{1}[f(s) \geq \tau], y) = \argmax_\tau \text{UAR}(\mathbf{1}[s \geq f^{-1}(\tau)], y)$. So the isotonic-calibrated UAR-at-optimal-$\tau$ should equal the pre-isotonic UAR-at-optimal-$\tau$. The observed $-0.0026$ delta is a $\tau$-grid resolution artefact that vanishes if the grids are unified (e.g., by transforming the optimal raw $\tau^*$ through the isotonic mapping and using that as the calibrated $\tau$ directly). Strict M15 statement holds: monotonic calibration cannot \emph{improve} UAR-at-optimal-$\tau$, only \emph{equal} or appear-\emph{worse} due to discretisation.

\textbf{M16 diagnostic.} The learned stack assigns two negative seed weights and large coefficients, improves train\_threshold, and worsens historical devel\_test. This pattern is consistent with selection overfit in this small calibration sample; it does not establish a universal stacking mechanism.

\section{K=3 with HuBERT-base + learned LW softmax (follow-up)}
\label{app:k3_hubert_lw}

We trained an A2.5-style HuBERT-base head with the same five-seed layer-audit and cold-CE recipe as the WavLM head. This tests whether learned layer weighting improves HuBERT mean pooling; it does not isolate backbone capacity or fusion redundancy.

\paragraph{Per-layer HuBERT-base audit.} L0 has the highest historical sub@1 score ($+0.0525$). Per-layer cold UAR spans $0.5569$--$0.6128$ and historical proxy-probe top-1 spans $0.045$--$0.078$. These observations motivate learned layer weighting but do not identify how cold or true-speaker information is distributed.

\paragraph{HuBERT-A2.5 head training.} All 5 seeds converged to similar layer-weight distributions: $\cos$(audit prior, final softmax) $= 0.9489$--$0.9500$ across seeds; top-5 final layers identical across seeds (L0 $\approx 0.31$, L1 $\approx 0.084$, L12 $\approx 0.083$, L10 $\approx 0.069$, L3 $\approx 0.067$). The audit prior is tightly preserved through training, mirroring the M5 finding for WavLM-A2.5. Early stopping fired at epoch 1--4 for all seeds (the small layer-weight subspace + saturating cold signal makes additional epochs hurt rather than help; train loss drops fast $0.25 \to 0.01$ by epoch 7--10 but val UAR plateaus or declines after epoch 1--4 --- classic overfitting signature on the small head capacity).
