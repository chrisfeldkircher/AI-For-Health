\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Historical partition results and the non-comparable hidden-test reference}
\label{ssec:shadow_first}

All per-stage numbers in Table~\ref{tab:stack} are on the historical canonical
\texttt{devel\_test} partition. The 2017 ComParE Cold $0.710$ was a hidden-test
number and the hidden labels are unavailable, so no apples-to-apples comparison
exists. The shadow distribution quantifies sensitivity to the historical
partitioner; after the independent overlap audit it is no longer a binding
speaker-independent metric.

\textbf{Same-partition architecture contrast.} Across 10 replayed historical boundaries the multi-K ensemble attains UAR $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$ (K=2-only $0.6882 \pm 0.0169$), versus $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$ for ComParE-2016 $+$ LR. The observed $+0.109$ difference is valid under matched boundaries, but neither system is evaluated on a verified speaker-disjoint Development sub-split. Whole-official-side transfer is required for a load-bearing performance claim.

\textbf{The canonical--baseline comparison is not inferential.} The canonical system reaches $0.7111$ (K=2-only $0.7090$), numerically adjacent to the 2017 hidden-test $0.7100$, but the protocols and data differ and the historical Development boundary is not verified speaker-disjoint. We therefore make no equivalence, variance-transfer or leakage-attribution claim from that proximity.

\textbf{Historical refinement rule.} Multi-K and classical K=3 fusion were post-hoc refinements judged by the same pre-specified replay rule (paired lift $\geq0.003$ and $\geq7/10$ positive boundaries). Multi-K passes ($+0.006 \pm 0.004$, $9/10$); ComParE-K3 fails ($+0.00002 \pm 0.0026$, $5/10$). Applying one rule consistently is preferable to choosing the highest canonical point, but the rule still operates on the invalid historical proxy map. Multi-K is therefore the preferred exploratory refinement within that protocol, not a confirmed headline system.

\subsection{Exploratory-vs-confirmatory framing}

K=2 feature selection tested five candidates on \texttt{devel\_test} and selected G5\_modulation, so it is a best-of-five Development selection. Two additional model seeds at fixed G5 produced lifts inside the original three-seed range, showing model-seed repeatability on the same selected boundary. They are not confirmatory data and do not bound the selection effect, because the data and selection target are unchanged. The broader candidate and ablation trail is therefore exploratory; confirmation requires choices frozen before whole-side evaluation.

\subsection{Ensemble caveats}

The K=2 5-seed mean-logit ensemble is a single evaluation on the canonical historical boundary; its $+0.005$ over the five per-seed mean costs 5$\times$ inference. The seed standard deviation of the single systems and the replay-boundary standard deviation answer different questions, and neither is uncertainty for a corrected whole-side score. Deployment claims require a frozen latency/performance comparison under the corrected protocol.

\subsection{Methodological caveat: the lr $\times$ init grid}

The A2.5 learning-rate stress test shows that the layer-weight subspace does move at high rates ($\cos(\text{init},\text{final})=0.83$ at lr$\times10$ and $0.21$ at lr$\times100$). In the tested lr-by-initialisation grid, uniform initialisation reaches UAR $0.6447$--$0.6466$, while the audit-derived initialisation reaches $0.657$--$0.664$. This demonstrates different outcomes within the tested optimisation grid, not an unreachable global attractor. A2.5-default was retained because higher lr added $0.007$ UAR alongside $0.016$ higher historical proxy-probe accuracy; that trade-off must be rechecked with corrected groups.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textit{(L1) Historical proxy-probe sensitivity.} Under the exact $k=210$ historical proxy-label protocol, the strongest tested discriminator reaches only about $0.085$ top-1. This makes small probe changes difficult to interpret, especially because the held-side labels are fragmented. We therefore report discriminator ceiling and memo gap together and do not claim a corpus-wide speaker-probe noise floor.

\textit{(L2) Frozen-backbone architectural commitment.} We test no full-transformer fine-tune. The negative de-confounding results cover only the listed frozen-backbone configurations under the historical protocol; a full fine-tune or another head may differ. HuBERT-base mean pooling failed the pre-flight rule, while a learned layer-weighted HuBERT head cleared standalone screening but failed K=3 admission (Section~\ref{ssec:k3_hubert}, Appendix~\ref{app:k3_hubert_lw}). Redundancy with WavLM is a hypothesis, not a measured mechanism. HuBERT-large mean pooling remains untested.

\textit{(L3) ECAPA is supported as a speaker-correlated substrate; held-side proxy IDs are not interchangeable.} Side-local raw-space KMeans and HDBSCAN agree strongly on every official side (all-recording ARI $0.829$--$0.879$; non-noise sensitivity $0.929$--$0.944$ at $96.69$--$97.59\,\%$ coverage). However, Train-centroid assignments agree poorly with side-local labels on Development and Test and do not create speaker-proxy-disjoint held-side partitions. Proxy groups must therefore be fitted locally for within-side use, and cold labels must never select the representation, grouping method or granularity. Without ground-truth identities, method agreement supports the representation but cannot validate the labels as true speakers.

\textit{(L4) No hidden-test extrapolation.} Because the historical shadow boundaries are not verified speaker-disjoint, their $\sigma$ is not a defensible model for hidden-test partition variance. The matched ComParE difference remains a same-boundary architecture contrast, not a speaker-independent competence estimate.

\textit{(L5) The historical devel\_val/devel\_test boundary is not established speaker-disjoint.} Although the shipped IDs have zero overlap by construction, 201/210 independently recovered Development-local groups cross the canonical boundary and the cross-boundary nearest-neighbour rate is 27.46\,\%. Neither quantity estimates UAR inflation. A simplified downstream comparison moved by $+0.0036$ in favour of the corrected grouping rather than the historical grouping, so we retract the disjointness claim without attributing a numerical score gap to this defect. Early stopping, fusion selection and shadow results using this split remain exploratory; whole-side transfer or a corrected side-local split is required for binding evaluation.

\textit{(L6) Class-balanced sampling vs natural class distribution.} A2 / A2.5 / K=1 / K=2 all use balanced sampling. At the observed 9.5\,\% cold prevalence, operating-point trade-offs can differ from balanced training. The ensemble's recall shift is descriptive on the historical boundary and must be re-estimated on corrected whole-side evaluation.
