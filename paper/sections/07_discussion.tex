\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Shadow-first headline; the canonical--baseline comparison is one partition}
\label{ssec:shadow_first}

All per-stage numbers in Table~\ref{tab:stack} are on the canonical \texttt{devel\_test} (\texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold}, SPLIT\_SEED=42). The 2017 ComParE Cold $0.710$ was a hidden-test number with unspecified speaker-disjointness, and the hidden labels are unavailable, so no apples-to-apples comparison exists. We therefore make the shadow distribution, not the canonical point, the binding metric (§\ref{ssec:shadow_robustness}, Fig.~\ref{fig:shadow}).

\textbf{The honest estimate and the one assumption-free competence claim.} Across 10 alternative \texttt{(devel\_val, devel\_test)} partitions the multi-K ensemble attains shadow-mean UAR $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$ (K=2-only ensemble $0.6882 \pm 0.0169$). The single comparison free of partition-variance ambiguity is the \emph{same-protocol} one: a classical ComParE-2016 $+$ LR control evaluated under the identical speaker-grouped shadow harness attains $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$, so the frozen-model pipeline dominates the classical-feature-family architecture by $\Delta\,{+}0.109$ shadow-mean UAR --- same splits, same harness, no hidden-test extrapolation. This, not any canonical-vs-2017 statement, is the headline competence claim.

\textbf{The canonical--baseline comparison, stated as one partition.} On the single fixed canonical partition the system reaches $0.7111$ (K=2-only $0.7090$), adjacent to the 2017 hidden-test $0.7100$. We do not read this as matching or beating that baseline. Both are single-partition point estimates; the canonical sits at $z {=} +1.24\sigma$ (K=2-only) / $+1.09\sigma$ (multi-K) above its \emph{own} shadow mean --- a quantified favourable draw, not an expected-test position --- and contextualising the hidden-test number through our measured devel partition variance ($\sigma_{\text{shadow}} \approx 0.016$) \emph{assumes} the hidden-test split has comparable variance, which we cannot verify. Under that explicit assumption the canonical $0.7111$, the shadow mean $0.6940$, and the baseline $0.7100$ all fall within one partition $\sigma$ of one another; we claim no more than that. The $0.5853 \to 0.7100$ raw gap of $\Delta\,+0.125$ between the classical control and the 2017 number is the joint effect of our stricter speaker-grouped split (cross-speaker leakage closes) and the SVM-vs-LR architectural choice; the protocol-stricter split likely dominates, since SVM-vs-LR rarely yields $+0.10{+}$ UAR gaps at this data scale.

\textbf{Multi-K legitimacy: the same gate promotes one refinement and rejects the other.} Multi-K and the classical K=3 fusion are both \emph{post-hoc} refinements over the controlled A2$\to$K=2 ladder, judged by one pre-registered shadow-admission rule (paired shadow lift $\geq 0.003$ \emph{and} $\geq 7/10$ positive splits). Multi-K passes ($+0.006 \pm 0.004$, $9/10$ positive); the ComParE-K3 candidate fails ($+0.00002 \pm 0.0026$, $5/10$ --- a coin-flip) \emph{despite} reaching the highest single-partition canonical UAR in the project ($0.7126$, $\Delta\,+0.0026$ vs the 2017 number). The identical rule promotes one and rejects the other: multi-K is the headline configuration because it cleared the gate, not because it maximised the canonical point. Multi-K-with-K=3 is therefore paper-supplementary, and the gate's asymmetric verdict on two structurally similar refinements is itself a data point for the C2 evaluation protocol.

\subsection{Exploratory-vs-confirmatory framing}

The K=2 G\_other candidate selection (Section~\ref{ssec:k2}) tested 5 candidates $\{G1, G2, G3, G5, G6\}$ on devel\_test and locked G5\_modulation as the winner. Strict reading: this is a ``best-of-5'' devel-test selection. The 5-seed expansion (Section~\ref{ssec:k2_5seed}) at \emph{fixed} G5 on 2 unseen-during-selection seeds $\{999, 31337\}$ is confirmatory; the new seeds' K=2 lifts ($+0.0092$, $+0.0095$) sit within the original 3-seed range ($+0.0075$ to $+0.0137$), bounding the selection effect. We are explicit about this framing to avoid the multiple-comparison-on-devel critique. The broader ablation trail stays in the paper because it explains the reasoning behind the final configuration; it should be read as supporting evidence for the story, not as a set of equally weighted headline contributions. The exhaustive K=2 sweep across all 6 admitted A5a groups + the K=3 with full eGeMAPSv02 confirms G5 as the unique winner under the locked-$\beta$ methodology.

\subsection{Ensemble caveats}

The K=2 5-seed mean-logit ensemble is a single-number evaluation, not $\sigma$-bearing. The $\sigma$-bearing canonical headline stays the per-seed K=2 single mean (0.7037 $\pm$ 0.0060). The ensemble is a paper-supplementary ablation row showing $+0.005$ UAR from averaging at 5$\times$ inference cost. Per-seed single is the deployment-relevant number; the ensemble is the upper bound on this stack.

\subsection{Methodological honesty about lr $\times$ init grid}

The A2.5 lr stress test produced a clarification of the original ``flat landscape'' claim: the layer-weight subspace has gradient signal at lr$\times 10$ ($\cos(\text{init}, \text{final})$ drops to $0.83$ at lr$\times 10$, $0.21$ at lr$\times 100$). The lr $\times$ init grid (uniform vs honest-prior $\times$ lr$\times \{1, 3, 5, 10\}$, 24 retrains, 3 seeds per cell) shows the honesty prior is a \emph{distinct attractor} that uniform initialisation cannot reach via lr alone --- uniform plateaus at UAR $0.6447$--$0.6466$ across all tested lr while honesty-prior reaches $0.657$--$0.664$ at every lr. We report A2.5-default (honest-prior + lr$\times 1$) as canonical because higher-lr trades $+0.007$ UAR for $+0.016$ LR-probe leak --- a Pareto-bad trade for the de-confounding paper narrative. The grid serves as ablation evidence; the per-cell numbers and the cosine table across the lr-axis are in Appendix~\ref{app:lr_init_grid}.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textit{(L1) Speaker-probe noise floor at this corpus scale (M14).} URTIC at $k{=}210$ pseudo-speakers with $\sim$40 chunks/speaker produces a speaker-probe top-1 ceiling around 0.085 even with the strongest tractable discriminator. ``Probe top-1 dropped from $X$ to $Y$'' framings common in the de-confounding literature are potentially misleading on similar-scale corpora; we report disc-ceiling + memo gap alongside every probe number we cite. Future work on larger-corpus or fine-grained-speaker variants could quantify the tightening of the noise floor.

\textit{(L2) Frozen-backbone architectural commitment.} We test no full-transformer fine-tune. The negative de-confounding results therefore hold ``at every frozen-backbone scope tractable in budget,'' not ``at every architectural level full stop.'' A full fine-tune may give a different verdict for both contrastive (A6) and adversarial (A7) interventions. Documenting this scope limit is part of the project's epistemic honesty; we do not extrapolate beyond it. The A5b K=3 multi-FM probe was run for both HuBERT-base mean-pooled (M14 pre-flight fail) \emph{and} HuBERT-base with a learned layer-weighted softmax (cleared the standalone threshold but failed K=3 fusion by logit-correlation with the WavLM-A2.5 anchor; Section~\ref{ssec:k3_hubert}, Appendix~\ref{app:k3_hubert_lw}); the one structurally untested capacity variant is HuBERT-large mean-pooled, which remains future work.

\textit{(L3) Pseudo-speaker labels are a validated proxy, not ground-truth IDs.} The 4students release omits speaker IDs, so we use ECAPA-TDNN embeddings clustered into $k{=}210$ as the speaker-grouping proxy ($\sim$630 true speakers over three partitions $\Rightarrow$ $\sim$210 per partition). We validate the \emph{shipped} labels in their own raw L2-normalised ECAPA space (Fig.~\ref{fig:ecapa}): kNN label cohesion is $94\times$ above chance and an independent HDBSCAN run is non-degenerate and recovers a consistent finer partition (NMI $0.77$). This makes the labels a strong speaker-correlated proxy, but the leakage audit's validity ultimately rests on ECAPA being an independent speaker-verification substrate and on the functional reduction in recoverable speaker information --- not on near-perfect cluster recovery. A finer or coarser $k$ may shift probe ceilings; the M14 finding is that the qualitative pattern (memorisation dominates) is stable across $k$.

\textit{(L4) No hidden-test access; the partition-variance contextualisation is an explicit, unverifiable assumption.} Every reported number is devel-side. The canonical-vs-2017 framing (§\ref{ssec:shadow_first}) treats the hidden-test $0.7100$ as a single-partition point comparable within our measured devel partition variance $\sigma_{\text{shadow}} \approx 0.016$; this \emph{assumes} the 2017 hidden-test split has comparable variance, which we cannot check. The same-protocol ComParE-2016$+$LR dominance ($\Delta\,{+}0.109$) is the claim that does \emph{not} depend on this assumption, and is the one we lead with.

\textit{(L5) Devel\_val sub-split for early stop is also speaker-disjoint from devel\_test, but not from training in the sense of having any cross-validation discipline.} The early-stop split was fixed at \texttt{val\_frac=0.50} with seed 42; a different seed/frac may slightly shift the locked-$\beta^*$ choices. The stability of $\beta^* \in \{6, 8\}$ across all 5 seeds for K=2 suggests this is a minor concern.

\textit{(L6) Class-balanced sampling vs natural class distribution.} A2 / A2.5 / K=1 / K=2 all use balanced sampling. The 9.5\,\% cold rate at deployment time may produce different operating-point trade-offs; the recall pattern shift in the ensemble (Section~\ref{ssec:ensemble}) suggests the K=2 ensemble is the most cold-recovery-friendly variant.
