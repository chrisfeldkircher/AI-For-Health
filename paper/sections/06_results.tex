\subsection{Multi-K ensemble under the historical partitioner}
\label{ssec:multi_k}

Multi-K averages the per-seed K=1 and K=2 fused logits at their locked $\beta^*_{k1},\beta^*_{k2}$ values, then averages across five seeds. Mathematically, $\text{multi\_K}_{\text{seed}}=0.5(K_1^{\text{seed}}+K_2^{\text{seed}})=a_{2.5}+0.5(\beta_{k1}+\beta_{k2}/2)z_{g4}+0.25\beta_{k2}z_{g5}$. It is therefore a reweighted K=2 construction with seed-specific channel weights; any variance benefit is an empirical within-protocol observation rather than an assumed property.

\textbf{Result.} On the canonical historical boundary, multi-K reaches $\mathbf{0.7111}$ versus $0.7090$ for K=2-only ($\Delta {+}0.0021$). Across the 10 replayed historical boundaries it reaches $\mathbf{0.6940 \pm 0.0157}$ versus $0.6882 \pm 0.0169$ (paired $\Delta {+}0.0059 \pm 0.0041$, positive on \textbf{9/10} boundaries). The small reduction in replay standard deviation is descriptive only: these boundaries reuse one official side and fragmented proxy groups, so it is not evidence about speaker-independent or hidden-test variance.

\textbf{Operating-point shift.} Multi-K's locked $\tau^* {=} -1.625$ vs K=2-only's $-1.375$ --- the asymmetric per-channel weighting (more on G4\_gi) pushes the operating point toward more cold-aggressive decisions, consistent with the K=1-component contributing additional cold-axis signal at the locked $\beta_{k1}$.

\textbf{Historical-protocol interpretation.} Multi-K averages existing K=1 and K=2 predictions and therefore adds no training cost. Its paired lift repeats on 9/10 replayed boundaries, so it is not unique to boundary 42 within the old partitioner. Because the same invalid held-side proxy map underlies all boundaries, this repetition does not establish independent-speaker transfer. Per-boundary paired differences are in Appendix~\ref{app:k2_shadow}.

\textbf{Diverse-anchor 10-seed ablation.} We also tested whether adding 5 hyperparameter-varied A2.5 heads (lr$\times 2$, lr$\times 0.5$, dropout 0.3, dropout 0.7, proj\_dim 256) to the canonical 5-seed pool would help. All 5 added heads converged but had weaker val\_UAR ($0.629$--$0.633$, versus $\sim 0.656$ for the canonical heads). The 10-seed multi-K ensemble gives $0.6920 \pm 0.0161$ across historical replays versus $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$ for 5 seeds (paired $\Delta -0.0021 \pm 0.0015$, positive on $1/10$; canonical $\Delta -0.0022$). Thus none of these five specific additions improved the historical result; this is not an exhaustive ensemble-size or hyperparameter optimum claim.

\textbf{Same feature-family LR control (ComParE-2016 + LR).} We evaluated the 6373-d ComParE-2016 feature family with a regularised cold-LR probe on the same historical partitions; this is not a replication of the original SVM. It reaches canonical UAR $0.5964$ and shadow-mean $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$, versus $0.6940$ for the frozen-model multi-K system. The $+0.109$ difference is a valid same-partition architecture comparison, but it does not resolve the apples-to-oranges comparison with the 2017 hidden test and cannot be attributed to closing cross-speaker leakage. Adding ComParE-LR as K=3 gives $0.7126$ on the canonical partition but essentially no shadow-mean lift ($+0.00002$, positive on $5/10$ partitions), so it remains supplementary.

\subsection{Non-comparable hidden-test reference}

The canonical $0.7111$, historical-replay mean $0.6940$, and 2017 hidden-test $0.7100$ arise from different data boundaries and evaluation protocols. The historical replay spread cannot be transferred to the hidden test after the overlap audit, so neither the $+0.001$ canonical difference nor the $-0.016$ replay-mean difference supports a match, win, expected-test estimate or uncertainty statement. The valid comparator in this study is the matched ComParE-2016$+$LR control evaluated on the same historical boundaries.

\subsection{Project-local standalone-UAR screening heuristic}
\label{ssec:standalone_predictor}

Across the eight project-selected candidates in Table~\ref{tab:standalone_predictor}, standalone cold-LR UAR was a useful screening signal: most candidates near or above $0.61$ used an interior $\beta^*$, whereas weaker candidates tended to be absorbed at a boundary $\beta^* \in \{12,16\}$. HuBERT-A2.5 is an observed exception. We therefore use standalone UAR only as a project-local compute-triage rule, not as a validated predictor of fusion admission.

\textbf{Observed across two substrate types, not externally generalised.} The candidate set contains handcrafted groups and two foundation-model-derived representations. That breadth makes the rule worth testing prospectively, but the small, adaptively selected sample cannot establish a universal threshold or cross-corpus transfer. A future test must freeze the threshold before evaluating new candidate families and report both false rejections and wasted sweeps.

\textbf{Within-project pre-flight use.} Before the HuBERT-base mean-pooled K=3 candidate was considered, we fixed a standalone-UAR floor of $0.55$ from the earlier K=2 sweep. HuBERT-base mean-pooled scored $0.5396$, so the full K=3 sweep was skipped, saving about 5 minutes. Because the skipped outcome is unobserved and the rule was derived from the same project, this demonstrates a reproducible compute policy rather than predictive validity.

\paragraph{Candidate refinement to test prospectively.} HuBERT-A2.5 cleared $0.61$ but did not admit to K=3 (5-seed $0.6955 \pm 0.0083$, $\Delta -0.008$ versus locked K=2; 4 of 5 seeds at boundary $\beta^*{=}16$). This falsifies standalone UAR as a sufficient admission rule. Redundant candidate and anchor rankings are one plausible explanation, but the required correlation diagnostic was not recorded. We therefore treat the following as hypotheses for a future frozen protocol:

\begin{itemize}
\item For recipe-orthogonal candidates, retain standalone UAR as a low-cost screening feature, without assuming a universal cutoff.
\item For candidates built by the same layer-audit recipe as the anchor, require both adequate standalone UAR and a frozen redundancy diagnostic.
\item Measure candidate--anchor logit correlation on \texttt{train\_threshold} before the $\beta$ sweep; any cutoff, including $r>0.8$, must be pre-specified and then validated rather than inferred retrospectively.
\end{itemize}

Recipe-orthogonal candidates, such as discrete-token histograms or a differently pooled larger HuBERT model, may provide less redundant information and remain future work (Section~\ref{sec:conc}). That possibility has not been tested here.

\subsection{Recall pattern flip in the ensemble}

On the historical canonical boundary, the 5-seed mean-logit ensemble produces recC $=0.791$ and recNC $=0.627$ (versus per-seed single recC $\approx0.43$, recNC $\approx0.87$). The same cached prediction path reproduces this operating-point shift, but its practical value must be reassessed on a corrected whole-side evaluation.

\subsection{Aggregation and test-time augmentation under the historical protocol}
\label{ssec:ensemble_defence}

We compared four aggregation rules and a small test-time augmentation (TTA) set. All variants use the same 4795-chunk historical pseudo-grouped \texttt{devel\_test} sub-split, with $\tau$ swept on \texttt{train\_threshold}. Mean-logit at $0.7090$ is the within-protocol reference; full tables are in Appendices~\ref{app:k2_calibration} and~\ref{app:k2_tta}.

\textbf{Calibration / stacked weighting (M15 + M16).} We tested four reweighting strategies on the per-seed K=2 fused-logit matrix $[N_{\text{chunks}},5]$: equal-weight mean-logit; class-balanced logistic stacking; a 7776-point UAR weight grid; and isotonic calibration. Logistic stacking scored $0.6137$ ($\Delta-0.0953$), the grid-selected weights scored $0.7049$ ($\Delta-0.0041$), and isotonic calibration was $0.0026$ below mean-logit. Thus equal-weight mean-logit was best among these four choices on this historical boundary. The result does not establish a global variance optimum or corrected-side performance.

\textbf{Test-time augmentation (M17).} We tested the original audio plus $\pm2\%$ time stretch and $\pm2$\,dB gain, re-extracted through WavLM-Large, and averaged 25 logits per chunk. TTA scored \textbf{$0.6683$ ($\Delta-0.0407$)}. The gain variants were identical to four decimals, consistent with per-utterance input normalisation. Time stretch reduced mean per-seed UAR by $0.029$--$0.044$; temporal distribution shift and mismatch with unaugmented G4/G5 logits are plausible contributors but were not isolated experimentally. The supported conclusion is only that this particular TTA recipe hurt under the historical protocol.

\textbf{Within-protocol summary.} Mean-logit outperformed the tested mean-probability, learned-weight, grid-weight and isotonic alternatives, while the tested TTA recipe hurt. Reproducing $0.7090$ from the original cached predictions verifies the implemented aggregation path for those artifacts; it does not validate the historical grouping boundary or make the 2017 hidden-test value comparable.

\subsection{Historical partition replay --- canonical 0.7090 is high within the old procedure}
\label{ssec:shadow_robustness}

Every locked decision in the controlled-positive pipeline (per-seed $\beta^*$, thresholds, K=2 feature selection and the M14 screening threshold) was exposed to the canonical SPLIT\_SEED=42 Development partition. We replayed the locked configuration across 10 alternative boundaries produced by the same historical \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} procedure, keeping model and fusion choices fixed. Per-seed A2.5/G4/G5 logits were computed once on all 9596 Development chunks and repartitioned by file index. This tests boundary sensitivity within the old proxy map, not speaker-disjoint generalisation.

\subsection{Historical proxy-group sparsity and aggregation diagnostics}
\label{ssec:cold_sparsity}

A targeted diagnostic on the seed=23 outlier counted five \emph{historical pseudo-groups} containing cold chunks in devel\_test, versus ten on the canonical split. The side-local overlap audit shows that these cannot be interpreted as counts of distinct held-out speakers. The associated speaker-aggregated UAR is therefore an exploratory grouping sensitivity analysis, not evidence that particular independent speakers caused the variance.

Under the historical proxy map, speaker-aggregated UAR is more variable than chunk-UAR ($\sigma=0.082$ versus $0.017$). Because held-side IDs are fragmented, this does not validate a general speaker-level metric conclusion and does not rule out multi-chunk or speaker-level interventions. Official chunk-UAR remains the submission metric for the independent reason that it is the challenge metric.

\textbf{Smoothing over historical proxy groups hurts.} Interpolating chunk predictions with the shipped pseudo-group mean decreases UAR monotonically: full smoothing gives $0.6199$ versus $0.7090$ without smoothing, with paired historical-shadow difference $-0.096 \pm 0.015$. Because the held-side IDs are fragmented, this result applies only to smoothing over those proxy groups; it does not identify a within-true-speaker mechanism or rule out smoothing with verified identities.

The corrected M19 lesson is narrower: do not interpret proxy-group aggregation as subject-level analysis until the held-side assignments themselves have passed an independent overlap audit.
