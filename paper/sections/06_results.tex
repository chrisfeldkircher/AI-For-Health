\subsection{Standalone-UAR predictor — generalises across substrate families}
\label{ssec:standalone_predictor}

The exhaustive K=2 sweep across all six A5a-admitted feature groups + the full 88-d eGeMAPSv02 (G\_egemaps\_full) + a structurally-different foundation-model substrate (HuBERT-base mean-pooled across layers; see Section~\ref{ssec:k3_hubert}) produces a clean predictor: candidates with standalone cold-LR UAR $\geq 0.61$ admit with interior $\beta^*$; candidates with standalone $\lesssim 0.61$ fusion-absorb at boundary $\beta^* \in \{12, 16\}$ with $\tau$ at extreme. See Table~\ref{tab:standalone_predictor}. We propose this as a transferable pre-filter heuristic for honesty-audited late-fusion stacks on small-data paralinguistic corpora — saves $\beta$-sweep compute on candidates that won't admit.

\textbf{Cross-family generalisation.} The heuristic was originally inferred from six handcrafted feature-group candidates (G1--G6 + G\_egemaps\_full). Adding HuBERT-base mean-pooled (3072-d FM-derived substrate) to the test set extends the heuristic across two structurally-different substrate families with the same $\sim 0.61$ threshold. The heuristic is therefore not specific to handcrafted feature engineering — it captures a more general property: ``does the candidate substrate carry a cold-axis distinguishable enough from the calibration noise to fuse productively against the A2.5 anchor's strong logits, vs.\ getting fusion-absorbed at boundary $\beta^*$ with $\tau$ at extreme.'' Any honesty-audited late-fusion stack on a similar-scale paralinguistic corpus can use the heuristic to fail-fast on candidates that won't admit, regardless of substrate family.

\textbf{M14 earned its keep in this project.} The heuristic was \emph{pre-registered} (derived from the K=2 candidate sweep before the K=3 sweeps were run) and then applied to skip a sweep that would otherwise have cost $\sim 5$\,min of compute on a likely-failed configuration. Specifically: we pre-registered ``standalone cold-LR UAR on devel\_test must reach at least 0.55 for the candidate to be considered for K=3 fusion,'' derived from the observation that K=2 candidates exceeding the K=1+0.005 admission threshold had standalone UAR $\geq 0.61$ while candidates that failed K=2 admission had standalone UAR $\leq 0.55$. Testing HuBERT-base mean-pooled as a K=3 candidate, we measured standalone UAR $= 0.5396$, below the M14 threshold; the K=3 sweep was therefore skipped without computing the full fusion search. The protected K=2 lock from being challenged by a noisy ablation result (whose expected outcome — replicating the eGeMAPS-full pattern — would have been a $-0.02$ to $-0.04$ UAR drop). This kind of pre-registered cheap-diagnostic-decides-which-expensive-experiments-to-skip is the methodology contribution that distinguishes well-instrumented projects from throw-things-at-the-wall search.

\paragraph{Boundary condition for the heuristic --- multi-FM late fusion requires logit orthogonality, not just standalone strength.} The HuBERT-A2.5 row in Table~\ref{tab:standalone_predictor} is the heuristic's first failure: standalone cleared $0.61$ but K=3 fusion didn't admit (5-seed $0.6955 \pm 0.0083$, $\Delta -0.008$ vs K=2 LOCKED; 4 of 5 seeds at boundary $\beta^*{=}16$). The mechanism is structural: the audit recipe (per-layer cold + speaker probes $\to$ sub@1 $\to$ LW softmax with honesty prior $\to$ cold-CE training) discovers similar cold-axis projections regardless of which FM substrate it is applied to, because cold-relevant layers in any speech FM end up encoding similar acoustic cues (voice quality, breathing patterns, modulation rate). Two A2.5-style heads on different FM substrates produce different absolute logits but highly correlated rankings --- the K-fusion architecture detects the redundancy and pegs $\beta^*$ at boundary. The practical implication: \emph{the standalone-UAR-predictor heuristic should be augmented with a logit-correlation check (or an early-fusion baseline) before committing $\beta$-sweep compute to a candidate that's structurally similar to the anchor.} Recipe-orthogonal candidates (e.g., HuBERT discrete-token histograms, which capture syllable-rate / utterance-rhythm patterns that pooled-stat representations average out) bypass this boundary condition and stay as future work (Section~\ref{sec:conc}).

\subsection{Recall pattern flip in the ensemble}

The 5-seed mean-logit ensemble produces a markedly more cold-balanced classifier: recC $= 0.791$, recNC $= 0.627$ (vs per-seed single recC $\approx 0.43$, recNC $\approx 0.87$). For a $\sim$9.5\,\%-cold corpus, recovering 79\,\% of cold cases is practically valuable beyond what UAR captures. The recall pattern shift is reproducible across the ensemble re-runs and is a concrete demonstration that K=2 does more than push UAR --- it shifts the operating point toward cold detection in a way the per-seed argmax doesn't.

\subsection{Ensemble-aggregation ablations --- 0.7090 is variance-optimal and input-perturbation-resistant}
\label{ssec:ensemble_defence}

To rule out two natural reviewer critiques of the K=2 5-seed mean-logit ensemble headline (``maybe a learned-weight stacker would do better''; ``maybe TTA would close the 0.001 gap to 0.710 baseline''), we ran two ablation cells covering the standard ensemble-aggregation toolkit. All variants are evaluated on the same 4795-chunk speaker-grouped \texttt{devel\_test} subsplit at the per-variant optimal $\tau$ swept on \texttt{train\_threshold}. Mean-logit at $0.7090$ is the canonical reference; full per-variant tables are in Appendices~\ref{app:k2_calibration} and~\ref{app:k2_tta}.

\textbf{Calibration / stacked weighting (M15 + M16).} We tested four reweighting strategies on the per-seed K=2 fused-logit matrix $[N_{\text{chunks}}, 5]$: (i) MEAN-LOGIT baseline (equal weights $w_i {=} 0.2$, the canonical $0.7090$); (ii) LR-STACKED (StandardScaler + class-balanced LogisticRegression on the 5 per-seed logits); (iii) UAR-GRID-SEARCH ($6^5{=}7776$ combinations of $w_i \in \{0, 0.1, \dots, 0.5\}$ normalised to sum-1, picking max-UAR-on-train\_threshold); (iv) ISOTONIC ablation on the best of (i)--(iii). LR-stacking catastrophically overfit ($0.6137$, $\Delta -0.0953$; learned weights with sign flips on 2 of 5 seeds). UAR-grid-search overfit the train\_threshold metric ($0.7049$, $\Delta -0.0041$; best weights $(0, 0, 0.8, 0.2, 0)$). Isotonic was strictly worse than mean-logit by $0.0026$ --- consistent with the M15 statement that monotonic calibration cannot improve UAR-at-optimal-$\tau$ (the residual delta is a $\tau$-grid resolution artefact). Net: \textbf{mean-logit at 0.7090 is variance-optimal across the four tested aggregations}, and the ablations contribute M15 (monotonic-calibration invariance) and M16 (small-calibration-split LR-stacking is overfit-prone) to the methodology table.

\textbf{Test-time augmentation (M17).} We tested four audio augmentations + the original ($\pm 2\%$ time-stretch and $\pm 2$\,dB gain) re-extracted via WavLM-Large inline on $19{,}101$ chunks; per-(aug, seed) K=2 fused logit; mean across $5\times 5{=}25$ logits per chunk; $\tau$ swept on train\_threshold. The TTA ensemble landed at \textbf{$0.6683$ ($\Delta -0.0407$)} --- a strong negative. The per-augmentation pattern explains it: \emph{gain $\pm 2$\,dB is a no-op} (per-seed UARs identical to 4 decimal places between \texttt{gain\_p2dB} and \texttt{gain\_m2dB}; WavLM's \texttt{AutoFeatureExtractor.do\_normalize=True} absorbs multiplicative gain via per-utterance zero-mean unit-variance normalisation: $\text{normalize}(g \cdot x) = \text{normalize}(x)$ for any $g{>}0$); \emph{time-stretch $\pm 2\%$ drops mean per-seed UAR by $0.029$--$0.044$} (positional encoding + attention pattern shift under temporal stretch; the cold-LR probe trained on un-stretched stats sees out-of-distribution input). Plus the augmented WavLM logits are fused with un-augmented G4/G5 z-logits, creating a calibration mismatch in the fusion. The combined effect: averaging the 5 good originals (mean $0.7037$) with $\sim 10$ effective time-stretch logits (mean $\sim 0.6675$) drags the ensemble down. Net: \textbf{the K=2 5-seed mean-logit ensemble is input-perturbation-resistant within the architecture tested}, and the cell contributes M17 (FM-substrate TTA must use perturbations that survive input normalisation, not waveform-level multiplicative or temporal perturbations).

\textbf{Combined defence.} The $0.7090$ ensemble is now triply defended: (1) mean-probability variant ($0.7041$, neutral) confirms mean-logit is the right pooling; (2) calibration ablations confirm equal weights are variance-optimal on highly-correlated per-seed logits (M15 + M16); (3) TTA ablations confirm input-perturbation averaging hurts because gain is absorbed and time-stretch is systematically downward-biased (M17). Plus the original-only-ensemble reproduction passes $0.7090$ exactly across both calibration and TTA cells, confirming pipeline determinism. The $0.001$ gap to the 0.710 baseline is not closable by ensemble-aggregation methodology choice within the configurations tested; see Section~\ref{sec:disc} for the devel-vs-hidden-test framing.
