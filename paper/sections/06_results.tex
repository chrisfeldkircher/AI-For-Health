\subsection{Multi-K ensemble and baseline comparison}
\label{ssec:multi_k}

Variance reduction across the K-axis: average per-seed K=1 + K=2 fused logits at each seed's locked $\beta^*_{k1}, \beta^*_{k2}$, then 5-seed mean-logit ensemble of the per-seed averages. Mathematically, $\text{multi\_K}_{\text{seed}} = 0.5 \cdot (K_1^{\text{seed}} + K_2^{\text{seed}}) = a_{2.5} + 0.5(\beta_{k1} + \beta_{k2}/2) \cdot z_{g4} + 0.25 \beta_{k2} \cdot z_{g5}$ --- a re-weighted K=2 with asymmetric per-channel $\beta$ coefficients (more weight on G4\_gi than G5\_modulation) that differ across seeds. Five distinct effective per-channel weightings produce slightly less inter-seed correlation than 5 K=2-only seeds at uniform fusion weight.

\textbf{Result.} Multi-K canonical UAR $\mathbf{0.7111}$ (vs K=2-only $0.7090$, canonical $\Delta {+}0.0021$; vs 2017 hidden-test baseline $0.7100$, canonical $\Delta {+}0.0011$ --- a slight canonical-split edge that is interpreted more carefully below). Shadow-mean UAR $\mathbf{0.6940 \pm 0.0157}$ (vs K=2-only $0.6882 \pm 0.0169$, paired $\Delta {+}0.0059 \pm 0.0041$, positive on \textbf{9/10} shadow splits). Shadow $\sigma$ decreased slightly from $0.0169$ to $0.0157$, consistent with variance reduction. The shadow gap to baseline closes by $\sim 27\%$ from $-0.022$ (K=2-only) to $-0.016$ (multi-K), still within $\sim 1\sigma_{\text{shadow}}$ of the partition variance.

\textbf{Operating-point shift.} Multi-K's locked $\tau^* {=} -1.625$ vs K=2-only's $-1.375$ --- the asymmetric per-channel weighting (more on G4\_gi) pushes the operating point toward more cold-aggressive decisions, consistent with the K=1-component contributing additional cold-axis signal at the locked $\beta_{k1}$.

\textbf{Justification.} Multi-K is a textbook ensemble diversification trick (averaging across hyperparameter variations, here the K dimension) at zero training cost (uses existing locked $\beta_{k1}, \beta_{k2}$ from the per-seed canonical sweep). The shadow-eval gate confirms the lift is not a canonical-favorability artifact: 9/10 shadow splits positive, paired-$\Delta$ std $0.0041$ much smaller than the shadow $\sigma$ of either baseline. Per-shadow-split paired-$\Delta$ table in Appendix~\ref{app:k2_shadow}.

\textbf{Ensemble-size optimisation closure (diverse-anchor 10-seed ablation).} We also tested whether adding 5 hyperparameter-varied A2.5 heads (lr$\times 2$, lr$\times 0.5$, dropout 0.3, dropout 0.7, proj\_dim 256) to the canonical 5-seed pool would lift the ensemble via increased anchor diversity. All 5 diverse-anchor heads converged but with individually weaker val\_UAR ($0.629$--$0.633$, vs the canonical 5 seeds' $\sim 0.656$, $\Delta -0.023$ to $-0.027$). The 10-seed diverse multi-K ensemble shadow-mean is $0.6920 \pm 0.0161$ vs the 5-seed multi-K $0.6940 \pm 0.0157$: paired $\Delta -0.0021 \pm 0.0015$, positive on $1/10$ shadow splits. Canonical $\Delta -0.0022$. Decisive negative; both directions agree. The canonical hyperparameters (lr$=10^{-3}$, dropout $=0.5$, proj\_dim $=128$) are at or near the local optimum --- each departure produces weaker individual heads, and ensemble averaging can't recover from weaker base predictors. The 5-seed multi-K ensemble is the locally-optimal canonical-recipe ensemble. \emph{This is a defensive ablation supporting the 5-seed ensemble size as adequate.}

\textbf{Classical-baseline replication (ComParE-2016 + LR) --- resolves the apples-to-oranges devel-vs-hidden-test caveat.} We tested the 2017 ComParE Cold sub-challenge baseline architecture (6373-d ComParE-2016 functionals via openSMILE + regularised cold-LR cold probe) under our speaker-grouped subsplit discipline. \textbf{Standalone canonical UAR $0.5964$; shadow-mean $0.5853 \pm 0.0083$.} This is meaningfully below both FM-A2.5 standalone ($\sim 0.656$) and the 2017 hidden-test baseline ($0.7100$). The $0.5853 \to 0.7100$ gap ($+0.125$) is the joint effect of (i) the speaker-grouped subsplit being stricter than the 2017 hidden-test split (cross-speaker leakage closes) and (ii) the SVM-vs-LR architectural choice plus feature normalisation differences. The protocol-stricter split likely dominates: SVM-vs-LR architectural differences rarely produce $+0.10+$ UAR gaps on this scale of data. \textbf{The FM-based system's lift over the classical baseline architecture on the same protocol is therefore $\Delta\,+0.109$ shadow-mean (multi-K $0.6940$ vs ComParE-LR $0.5853$).} Adding ComParE-LR as a K=3 fusion candidate (multi-K-with-K=3) bumps canonical UAR to \textbf{$0.7126$} (highest single-partition canonical achieved; exceeds 2017 baseline by $+0.0026$) but shadow-mean is essentially unchanged ($0.6941 \pm 0.0166$, paired $\Delta {+}0.00002$, positive on $5/10$ shadow splits = coin-flip). Per-seed locked $\beta_{k3}^* \in \{8, 12, 16, 16, 16\}$ ($4/5$ at boundary $16$) is the fusion-absorption signature seen in §\ref{ssec:k3_hubert} for low-standalone-UAR candidates --- ComParE-LR is too weak to provide a meaningful K=3 lift. The K=3-only ensemble (without the K=1+K=2 components) is $0.6746$ shadow-mean, meaningfully worse than 5-seed multi-K's $0.6940$. \emph{Multi-K-with-K=3 canonical $0.7126$ is paper-supplementary; the 5-seed multi-K stays canonical headline.}

\subsection{Baseline comparison under partition variance}

The multi-K 5-seed mean-logit ensemble at canonical $0.7111$ is $\Delta\,+0.001$ above the 2017 baseline of $0.7100$ on the canonical split; the multi-K shadow-mean $0.6940$ is $\Delta\,-0.016$ below the baseline ($\sim 1\sigma_{\text{shadow}}$). Both numbers are partition-variance-equivalent to the 2017 baseline (which is itself a single-partition point estimate with unknown but plausibly similar partition variance $\sigma \sim 0.017$). We discuss the framing carefully in Section~\ref{sec:disc}.

\subsection{Standalone-UAR predictor — generalises across substrate families}
\label{ssec:standalone_predictor}

The exhaustive K=2 sweep across all six A5a-admitted feature groups + the full 88-d eGeMAPSv02 (G\_egemaps\_full) + a structurally-different foundation-model substrate (HuBERT-base mean-pooled across layers; see Section~\ref{ssec:k3_hubert}) produces a clean predictor: candidates with standalone cold-LR UAR $\geq 0.61$ admit with interior $\beta^*$; candidates with standalone $\lesssim 0.61$ fusion-absorb at boundary $\beta^* \in \{12, 16\}$ with $\tau$ at extreme. See Table~\ref{tab:standalone_predictor}. We propose this as a transferable pre-filter heuristic for honesty-audited late-fusion stacks on small-data paralinguistic corpora — saves $\beta$-sweep compute on candidates that won't admit.

\textbf{Cross-family generalisation.} The heuristic was originally inferred from six handcrafted feature-group candidates (G1--G6 + G\_egemaps\_full). Adding HuBERT-base mean-pooled (3072-d FM-derived substrate) to the test set extends the heuristic across two structurally-different substrate families with the same $\sim 0.61$ threshold. The heuristic is therefore not specific to handcrafted feature engineering — it captures a more general property: ``does the candidate substrate carry a cold-axis distinguishable enough from the calibration noise to fuse productively against the A2.5 anchor's strong logits, vs.\ getting fusion-absorbed at boundary $\beta^*$ with $\tau$ at extreme.'' Any honesty-audited late-fusion stack on a similar-scale paralinguistic corpus can use the heuristic to fail-fast on candidates that won't admit, regardless of substrate family.

\textbf{M14 earned its keep in this project.} The heuristic was \emph{pre-registered} (derived from the K=2 candidate sweep before the K=3 sweeps were run) and then applied to skip a sweep that would otherwise have cost $\sim 5$\,min of compute on a likely-failed configuration. Specifically: we pre-registered ``standalone cold-LR UAR on devel\_test must reach at least 0.55 for the candidate to be considered for K=3 fusion,'' derived from the observation that K=2 candidates exceeding the K=1+0.005 admission threshold had standalone UAR $\geq 0.61$ while candidates that failed K=2 admission had standalone UAR $\leq 0.55$. Testing HuBERT-base mean-pooled as a K=3 candidate, we measured standalone UAR $= 0.5396$, below the M14 threshold; the K=3 sweep was therefore skipped without computing the full fusion search. The protected K=2 lock from being challenged by a noisy ablation result (whose expected outcome — replicating the eGeMAPS-full pattern — would have been a $-0.02$ to $-0.04$ UAR drop). This kind of pre-registered cheap-diagnostic-decides-which-expensive-experiments-to-skip is the methodology contribution that distinguishes well-instrumented projects from throw-things-at-the-wall search.

\paragraph{Substrate-conditional refinement of the heuristic.} The HuBERT-A2.5 row in Table~\ref{tab:standalone_predictor} is the original heuristic's first failure: standalone cleared $0.61$ but K=3 fusion didn't admit (5-seed $0.6955 \pm 0.0083$, $\Delta -0.008$ vs K=2 LOCKED; 4 of 5 seeds at boundary $\beta^*{=}16$). The mechanism is structural: the audit recipe (per-layer cold + speaker probes $\to$ sub@1 $\to$ LW softmax with honesty prior $\to$ cold-CE training) discovers similar cold-axis projections regardless of which FM substrate it is applied to, because cold-relevant layers in any speech FM end up encoding similar acoustic cues (voice quality, breathing patterns, modulation rate). Two A2.5-style heads on different FM substrates produce different absolute logits but highly correlated rankings --- the K-fusion architecture detects the redundancy and pegs $\beta^*$ at boundary. We propose the following substrate-conditional refinement of the heuristic:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Independent-substrate candidates} (handcrafted features, signal-processing groups, anything not derived through the same audit recipe as the anchor): standalone $\geq 0.55$--$0.61$ admit floor as originally calibrated.
\item \textbf{Correlated-substrate candidates} (other FM-derived heads built via the same audit recipe as the anchor; in our case any A2.5-style LW head trained on a different FM substrate): standalone $\geq$ \emph{the anchor's own standalone UAR} ($\sim 0.66$ for WavLM-A2.5). HuBERT-A2.5 at $0.6217 < 0.66$ would have been correctly pre-registered as a likely K=3 negative under this refined version.
\item \textbf{Ambiguous cases:} run an early-fusion correlation check (Pearson correlation of per-chunk candidate logit vs anchor logit on \texttt{train\_threshold}) before committing $\beta$-sweep compute. Candidates with $r > 0.8$ should be treated as correlated.
\end{itemize}

Recipe-orthogonal candidates (e.g., HuBERT discrete-token histograms, which capture syllable-rate / utterance-rhythm patterns that pooled-stat representations average out by construction; or HuBERT-large mean-pooled, which uses a different pooling protocol) bypass this boundary condition and stay as future work (Section~\ref{sec:conc}).

\subsection{Recall pattern flip in the ensemble}

The 5-seed mean-logit ensemble produces a markedly more cold-balanced classifier: recC $= 0.791$, recNC $= 0.627$ (vs per-seed single recC $\approx 0.43$, recNC $\approx 0.87$). For a $\sim$9.5\,\%-cold corpus, recovering 79\,\% of cold cases is practically valuable beyond what UAR captures. The recall pattern shift is reproducible across the ensemble re-runs and is a concrete demonstration that K=2 does more than push UAR --- it shifts the operating point toward cold detection in a way the per-seed argmax doesn't.

\subsection{Ensemble-aggregation ablations --- 0.7090 is variance-optimal and input-perturbation-resistant}
\label{ssec:ensemble_defence}

To rule out two natural reviewer critiques of the K=2 5-seed mean-logit ensemble headline (``maybe a learned-weight stacker would do better''; ``maybe TTA would close the 0.001 gap to 0.710 baseline''), we ran two ablation cells covering the standard ensemble-aggregation toolkit. All variants are evaluated on the same 4795-chunk speaker-grouped \texttt{devel\_test} subsplit at the per-variant optimal $\tau$ swept on \texttt{train\_threshold}. Mean-logit at $0.7090$ is the canonical reference; full per-variant tables are in Appendices~\ref{app:k2_calibration} and~\ref{app:k2_tta}.

\textbf{Calibration / stacked weighting (M15 + M16).} We tested four reweighting strategies on the per-seed K=2 fused-logit matrix $[N_{\text{chunks}}, 5]$: (i) MEAN-LOGIT baseline (equal weights $w_i {=} 0.2$, the canonical $0.7090$); (ii) LR-STACKED (StandardScaler + class-balanced LogisticRegression on the 5 per-seed logits); (iii) UAR-GRID-SEARCH ($6^5{=}7776$ combinations of $w_i \in \{0, 0.1, \dots, 0.5\}$ normalised to sum-1, picking max-UAR-on-train\_threshold); (iv) ISOTONIC ablation on the best of (i)--(iii). LR-stacking catastrophically overfit ($0.6137$, $\Delta -0.0953$; learned weights with sign flips on 2 of 5 seeds). UAR-grid-search overfit the train\_threshold metric ($0.7049$, $\Delta -0.0041$; best weights $(0, 0, 0.8, 0.2, 0)$). Isotonic was strictly worse than mean-logit by $0.0026$ --- consistent with the M15 statement that monotonic calibration cannot improve UAR-at-optimal-$\tau$ (the residual delta is a $\tau$-grid resolution artefact). Net: \textbf{mean-logit at 0.7090 is variance-optimal across the four tested aggregations}, and the ablations contribute M15 (monotonic-calibration invariance) and M16 (small-calibration-split LR-stacking is overfit-prone) to the methodology table.

\textbf{Test-time augmentation (M17).} We tested four audio augmentations + the original ($\pm 2\%$ time-stretch and $\pm 2$\,dB gain) re-extracted via WavLM-Large inline on $19{,}101$ chunks; per-(aug, seed) K=2 fused logit; mean across $5\times 5{=}25$ logits per chunk; $\tau$ swept on train\_threshold. The TTA ensemble landed at \textbf{$0.6683$ ($\Delta -0.0407$)} --- a strong negative. The per-augmentation pattern explains it: \emph{gain $\pm 2$\,dB is a no-op} (per-seed UARs identical to 4 decimal places between \texttt{gain\_p2dB} and \texttt{gain\_m2dB}; WavLM's \texttt{AutoFeatureExtractor.do\_normalize=True} absorbs multiplicative gain via per-utterance zero-mean unit-variance normalisation: $\text{normalize}(g \cdot x) = \text{normalize}(x)$ for any $g{>}0$); \emph{time-stretch $\pm 2\%$ drops mean per-seed UAR by $0.029$--$0.044$} (positional encoding + attention pattern shift under temporal stretch; the cold-LR probe trained on un-stretched stats sees out-of-distribution input). Plus the augmented WavLM logits are fused with un-augmented G4/G5 z-logits, creating a calibration mismatch in the fusion. The combined effect: averaging the 5 good originals (mean $0.7037$) with $\sim 10$ effective time-stretch logits (mean $\sim 0.6675$) drags the ensemble down. Net: \textbf{the K=2 5-seed mean-logit ensemble is input-perturbation-resistant within the architecture tested}, and the cell contributes M17 (FM-substrate TTA must use perturbations that survive input normalisation, not waveform-level multiplicative or temporal perturbations).

\textbf{Combined defence.} The $0.7090$ ensemble is now triply defended: (1) mean-probability variant ($0.7041$, neutral) confirms mean-logit is the right pooling; (2) calibration ablations confirm equal weights are variance-optimal on highly-correlated per-seed logits (M15 + M16); (3) TTA ablations confirm input-perturbation averaging hurts because gain is absorbed and time-stretch is systematically downward-biased (M17). Plus the original-only-ensemble reproduction passes $0.7090$ exactly across both calibration and TTA cells, confirming pipeline determinism. The $0.001$ gap to the 0.710 baseline is not closable by ensemble-aggregation methodology choice within the configurations tested; see Section~\ref{sec:disc} for the devel-vs-hidden-test framing.

\textbf{Verdict: canonical favorable but not pathological.} $z {=} +1.24\sigma$ above shadow mean falls in the ``marginal'' zone (within $2\sigma$ but above $1\sigma$). The locked configuration found a partition where its specific characteristics happened to align well with the test fold, plausibly via cumulative implicit fit through the per-seed locked $\beta^*$ on canonical \texttt{train\_threshold}, the ensemble locked $\tau^*$ on canonical \texttt{train\_threshold}, the K=2 G\_other selection on canonical \texttt{devel\_test}, and the M14 admission threshold calibrated on the canonical sweep. This is not methodological failure --- every decision followed the pre-registered protocol; the canonical split was fixed before any rung optimisation; the partition variance is the irreducible noise of single-partition evaluation on small-data corpora. The shadow distribution makes that noise quantitatively visible.

\textbf{Mean-logit ensemble lift transfers across partitions.} The mean-logit ensemble beats the per-seed-single 5-seed mean on 9 of 10 shadow splits (by $\Delta {+}0.001$ to ${+}0.004$), confirming the §\ref{ssec:ensemble} ensemble averaging mechanism is not a canonical-split artifact. The lift magnitude is roughly half the canonical $\Delta {+}0.0053$ on shadow splits on average --- partly canonical-favorability-dependent, partly genuine ensemble-variance-reduction.

\textbf{The 0.001 canonical-vs-baseline gap is much smaller than the partition variance.} Both our $0.7090$ canonical and the 2017 hidden-test baseline $0.7100$ are single-partition point estimates. If the 2017 baseline has comparable partition variance ($\sigma_{2017} \sim 0.017$ is plausible given similar corpus and protocol), it could plausibly land anywhere in $[0.68, 0.74]$ on a shadow split. The $0.001$ canonical-vs-baseline gap is much smaller than the partition $\sigma$ of either estimate; \emph{the systems are statistically equivalent within partition variance.} This is a stronger and more defensible claim than ``matches 0.710 within measurement noise'' because the analysis quantifies the canonical favorability ($+1.24\sigma$, $\Delta {+}0.021$) and frames the comparison in the right uncertainty units. Section~\ref{sec:disc} discusses the framing options and why we adopt the partition-variance equivalence as the canonical paper headline.

\textbf{Outlier and methodology contribution.} Per-shadow ensemble UAR is in $[0.6443, 0.7042]$. The high end (seed=11 at $0.7042$) is $\sim 1\sigma$ above shadow mean; the canonical at $0.7090$ slightly exceeds even the highest shadow split. The low outlier (seed=23 at $0.6443$) is $\sim 2.6\sigma$ below shadow mean --- the most extreme of the 10 splits. With 10 splits and $\sigma \sim 0.017$, the most extreme is expected to be $\sim 1.8\sigma$ from the mean by extreme-value statistics, so $-2.6\sigma$ once is plausible but slightly outside the 90\% CI; Appendix~\ref{app:k2_shadow} reports a per-pseudo-speaker diagnostic of this split. The methodology itself (shadow harness on cached pipeline logits as a cheap robustness diagnostic) is M18 in our methodology table (Section~\ref{ssec:m_disciplines}); we argue it should be a default reporting practice on small-data corpora where the held-out test set is unavailable.

\subsection{Cold-speaker sparsity dominates partition variance --- chunk-UAR is more stable than speaker-UAR on URTIC}
\label{ssec:cold_sparsity}

A targeted per-pseudo-speaker diagnostic on the seed=23 outlier (Appendix~\ref{app:k2_shadow}) reveals the structural mechanism: shadow\_seed=23's devel\_test contains only \textbf{5 cold speakers} (vs canonical's 10), and these specific cold speakers are harder-than-average for the locked configuration to classify (cold-speaker mean correctness $0.466$ vs canonical $0.694$). Speaker-level UAR collapses to $0.4056$ (vs canonical $0.7232$). The mechanism is structural but not a partition defect: cold speakers are sparse on URTIC ($\sim 21$ across the entire devel partition, $\sim 10$ per devel half), and seed=23 happened to put 5 specific cold speakers into devel\_test that were harder-than-average. The variance source is fundamentally about \emph{which} cold speakers land in test, not about chunk-level prediction quality.

This produces a counter-intuitive but load-bearing finding: \textbf{speaker-level UAR is far more variable than chunk-level UAR on URTIC.} Across the 10 shadow splits, chunk-UAR has $\sigma_{\text{shadow}} {=} 0.017$; speaker-UAR has $\sigma_{\text{shadow}} {=} 0.082$ ($\sim 5\times$ larger). The conventional paralinguistic intuition that speaker-level aggregation reduces noise does not hold here, because cold speakers are sparse: per-speaker accuracy is itself noisy, and aggregating $\sim 4800$ chunks down to $\sim 100$ speakers loses effective sample size faster than it removes per-chunk noise. \emph{This validates ComParE's choice of chunk-UAR as the official URTIC metric and rules out a class of related interventions} (multi-chunk inference, confidence weighting, speaker-level evaluation, multi-task speaker auxiliary heads).

\textbf{Speaker-level smoothing is uniformly bad.} As a direct corollary, we tested speaker-level prediction smoothing (interpolating between per-chunk and per-pseudo-speaker-mean predictions with weight $\alpha$). The $\alpha$-sweep is monotonically increasing toward $\alpha {=} 1.0$ (no smoothing) with no interior optimum: canonical UAR at $\alpha {=} 0.0$ is $0.6199$, climbing through $0.6400$, $0.6684$, $0.6852$, $0.6992$, $0.7052$, $0.7075$, $0.7080$, $0.7036$, $0.7047$, $0.7084$ to $0.7090$ at $\alpha {=} 1.0$. The shadow-paired $\Delta$ at $\alpha {=} 0$ is $-0.096 \pm 0.015$ (positive on $0/10$ shadow splits). Mechanism: URTIC cold detection works at the chunk level because \emph{within-speaker variation carries real cold evidence, not just noise} --- a single recording session contains chunks with coughs / throat clearing / breathiness (high cold evidence) interspersed with relatively clean speech (low cold evidence); the model's per-chunk predictions correctly assign different cold-probability based on local acoustic evidence, and speaker-mean smoothing averages these out. This is the opposite of the typical paralinguistic smoothing benefit ($+0.005$--$0.015$ UAR) and reflects a structural property of URTIC's chunk-level annotation protocol.

The unified methodology contribution (M19, Section~\ref{ssec:m_disciplines}): on corpora with sparse minority-class speaker representation, speaker-level aggregation increases variance rather than reducing it; the chunk-level metric is more stable; report both canonical and shadow-mean UARs to enable apples-to-apples comparison across studies on the same corpus.
